Project Idea: AI-Powered Ad Campaign Generator & Optimizer
Overview: Build a hackathon project that automatically generates and optimizes advertising campaigns using multiple AI services. The system will take a simple input (e.g. a product description or business idea) and instantly produce “magic” output – a set of ad creatives (text copy, possibly images), a campaign strategy (target audience, channels, budget allocation), and even an audio rendition of an ad via ElevenLabs. This delivers a wow-factor demo where a single click turns a business idea into a ready-to-run ad campaign within minutes.
Key Components and Technologies
Multi-Agent LLM Orchestration (Gemini + MCP): Leverage Google’s Gemini API as the core LLM for content generation, orchestrated by a multi-agent system (using Google’s ADK or similar) via an MCP server. Multiple specialized agents will handle different tasks in sequence (using the “agentic” pattern) – e.g. one agent for analyzing business context, one for generating ad copy, another for strategy recommendations. This sequential, multi-agent workflow ensures clear separation of concerns and modularity
medium.com
. For example, an orchestrator agent can chain a BusinessAnalysisAgent to distill the brand/product positioning, a ContentGenerationAgent to create tailored ad copies from that context, and a StrategyOptimizationAgent to plan channel budgets or timing. (In a similar Google Cloud demo, a CampaignOrchestratorAgent chained agents for business analysis, content generation, and even image creation
medium.com
.)
Knowledge Graph for Context: Structure the product and marketing info as a knowledge graph (or structured data in Snowflake) to ground the AI in facts about the brand. This is like building a mini knowledge graph for your brand – teaching the AI the connections between your product’s features, use cases, target audience, and even competitors
aimers.io
. The LLM agents can query this graph (e.g. via Snowflake or a graph DB) to retrieve accurate, up-to-date facts and ensure the generated ad content aligns with real product details and brand voice. Using a knowledge graph in retrieval augmeneted generation (RAG) provides richer context than text alone
medium.com
 – the system can pull not just isolated facts but the relationships between them, which helps the AI produce more contextually relevant and accurate ad copy.
Token Embeddings & Vector Search: Utilize vector embeddings to enable semantic search over past data or examples. Snowflake’s ML features (or an external vector DB) can store embeddings of successful ad copy, customer reviews, or campaign performance data. Embeddings capture semantic similarity (texts with similar meaning end up as vectors pointing in the same direction
docs.snowflake.com
docs.snowflake.com
). This means the system can take the new campaign idea and find similar marketing content or past ads (even if they don’t share keywords) to learn what worked. For example, given a product description, retrieve top-performing past ads for similar products and feed those as inspiration to the LLM. This knowledge-augmented generation ensures the AI’s output is grounded in what historically performs well. It also helps with personalization – matching ad tone to audience preferences via similarity of embeddings.
Deterministic Optimization Engine: Include a module for optimization to fine-tune campaign planning. This could be a linear programming or rule-based optimizer that allocates budgets or selects the best ad variants in a deterministic way (as opposed to purely random AI guesses). For instance, if the platform generates multiple ad copy variants and has performance estimates for each (perhaps predicted by an LLM or known industry benchmarks), the optimizer can choose the optimal combination of ad variant + channel + spend that maximizes an objective (e.g. expected clicks or conversions) under budget constraints. This is similar to how automated campaign budget optimization works – e.g. distributing spend to the best-performing ads
onrampfunds.com
 – but here you can formulate it as a solvable math problem. The result is a clear, data-driven campaign plan (which ad copy to run on which platform, at what budget) that the demo can show as “best recommended strategy.” By using deterministic algorithms for this step, the system’s recommendations will be transparent and repeatable, giving the demo an extra layer of credibility (you can explain the optimization logic).
ElevenLabs Voice Integration: To add an attention-grabbing multimodal element, use ElevenLabs API to convert the generated ad copy into a realistic voice narration. For example, if one of the outputs is a scripted video ad or radio jingle text, the system can instantly produce an audio clip of a human-like voice reading that ad. This component can be presented as “And here’s how your ad would sound on air!” Playing a short, AI-voiced advertisement to the judges can make the demo memorable. Technically, this involves taking the text output from the ContentGeneration agent and sending it to ElevenLabs’ text-to-speech API (using the credits on hand). You might allow the user to pick a voice or style (e.g. an upbeat male/female voice, etc.) to show some customization. The voice component underscores the “instant magic” – not only do you get copy and strategy, but even a produced asset ready to use.
Snowflake Data & Analytics: Snowflake can serve as the data backbone. Use Snowflake’s warehouse to store any relevant large datasets – e.g. a table of historical ad performance metrics, audience demographics, or product catalog info. The agents can query Snowflake (via Snowflake’s Python connector or Snowpark) to pull data for the campaign. For example, the BusinessAnalysisAgent might pull market data or past campaign results from Snowflake to inform the strategy (like which audience segment has the highest conversion rate for similar products). Snowflake could also hold the knowledge graph data (since Snowflake can integrate with graph libraries or even RelationalAI for GraphRAG). It also supports embedding generation and vector search internally
docs.snowflake.com
, so you might implement the semantic search for similar ads directly in Snowflake (leveraging their Cortex AI functions). This showcases using the Snowflake credits for heavy data analysis and ensures the AI has a strong factual grounding for its decisions.
Solana Integration (Optional): Since you have Solana credits and an LLM API access on Solana, you can incorporate a Web3 angle for bonus points. One idea: use Solana’s blockchain to store or verify campaign data – for example, mint an NFT representing the campaign plan or each ad creative (proving authenticity or timestamping the idea on-chain). Or use a Solana smart contract to handle an aspect of the ad buying (perhaps simulate executing ad spend from a crypto wallet). If Solana’s LLM API (via the Solana Agent or an on-chain inference service) is available, you could offload a specific task to it – e.g. have a Solana-based agent double-check compliance or do sentiment analysis on the ad copy (just to demonstrate multi-LLM interoperability). This part is not core to the ad functionality, but it leverages the credits and could be mentioned to judges as an innovative twist (e.g. “our system even logs the campaign to blockchain for transparency” or “we used Solana’s AI to verify the ad meets guidelines”). Keep this component simple due to hackathon time, but including it shows you made use of all available resources.
System Workflow (Step-by-Step)
User Input (Campaign Brief): The user provides a short brief – for example, “We sell eco-friendly running shoes for women, looking to target fitness enthusiasts in their 20s with an online campaign”. The demo could allow inputting a description, target audience, and maybe campaign goals (branding vs direct response).
Context Building (Knowledge Graph & Data Fetch): Immediately, the BusinessAnalysisAgent (or a similar module) kicks in. It uses the brief to gather context:
It might fetch structured facts from a knowledge base. For instance, it could query Snowflake for product details (shoe materials, price, USP) and any customer insights (perhaps sales data or reviews highlighting why customers love the product).
It could also utilize a Knowledge Graph of marketing information – e.g. connecting the product to related concepts (sustainability, running, health benefits) and the target audience’s interests. This graph could be pre-built or dynamically assembled. The agent ensures the AI knows “eco-friendly running shoes -> made of recycled material; target audience -> interests: marathons, gym, sustainability” etc.
Additionally, the agent can perform an embedding-based search in a reference dataset (maybe a library of successful ads or social media posts) to find semantically similar content. For example, find past ads for eco-friendly apparel or fitness products that performed well. These could be retrieved via vector similarity so that even if wording differs, the concept match is found. The retrieved examples or key phrases can be provided as inspiration or constraints to the generation step.
This step might also involve external info: e.g. querying trends (what’s trending with the audience) or ensuring no conflicting brand messages. The result of this phase is a compiled business context object (a structured summary of the brand position, audience, and key angles to hit). This context object can be used across all subsequent agents to keep them on the same page (a technique noted to harmonize outputs across text and visuals
medium.com
).
Ad Content Generation (Gemini LLM): Next, a ContentGenerationAgent uses the Gemini LLM (via its API) to create the actual ad creatives. It takes in the structured context from the previous step (brand info, key features, audience persona, plus example ads or keywords from embeddings) and prompts Gemini to generate copy for the campaign. Likely it will generate multiple items:
Ad copy variants for different channels: e.g. a couple of punchy one-liners for Twitter, a two-sentence ad for Facebook, a short paragraph for an email or blog intro, etc. Each can be tailored (Gemini can be prompted to adopt different tones or formats per channel).
Headline and Call-to-Action (CTA): e.g. a catchy headline and a CTA phrase (“Shop now for 20% off!”) relevant to the campaign goal.
If visuals were in scope, this agent might also produce prompts for image generation (since you mentioned only ElevenLabs and not image API, you might skip actual image gen. But if feasible, you could integrate an image model – e.g. use Imagen via Vertex AI if accessible – to create a product graphic or banner. In the Medium project
medium.com
, they used Imagen for “hero shots” of the product.)
The output here is the creative content. It’s “instant magic” for the user – within seconds, they see actual ad copies written for their product. You can highlight how the LLM infused the knowledge graph facts into the copy (ensuring accuracy) and used a style that matches the audience. For example, the Twitter ad might say “Run green! 🌱Step into sustainable style with our recycled-material runners. Light on your feet, lighter on the planet. #EcoRunning” – showing it understood the eco-friendly angle and the youthful tone.
Evaluation & Refinement: (This can be quick and behind-the-scenes for demo, but it’s a good tech element.) After generation, the content can be evaluated by another pass or agent:
Possibly use a critique agent (another LLM) or simple rules to check each ad variant for tone, compliance, or appeal. For instance, ensure no variant violates any policies or that it includes the key selling point from the knowledge graph. If something important is missing (say the knowledge graph noted “recyclable packaging” as a key value prop and the ad didn’t mention it), the agent could tweak or regenerate that variant.
If multiple variants were generated, the system can rank them. This is where embeddings can help again: embed each generated tagline and compare with the embedding of known high-performing slogans. Or use a sentiment/analyzer model to score how positive/inspiring the text is. The top-scoring ad copy might be marked as “recommended primary ad.”
The end of this step yields a set of finalized ad creatives that are optimized qualitatively (on-message and engaging). These will be presented to the user, possibly with an indication of which one is predicted to perform best.
Campaign Optimization (Budget/Channel Planning): With good ad content in hand, the StrategyOptimizationAgent (or just a separate function) tackles the question: how to deploy these ads for maximum impact. This is the deterministic optimization piece. The system can:
Propose the best channels/platforms for the ads (e.g. if target audience is young, prioritize Instagram/TikTok; professionals – LinkedIn, etc.). This can be rule-based (using demographic data from Snowflake or general marketing knowledge).
Allocate the budget across those channels and creatives. For a hackathon, you could simplify by assuming a fixed budget and splitting based on expected performance. However, to showcase optimization, formulate an example optimization problem: e.g. maximize reach = sum(impressions_per_dollar_channel * allocated_dollars_channel) subject to budget, and ensure each selected channel gets at least a minimum spend. Solve this with a straightforward algorithm or even linear programming if using Python (PuLP library) or an optimization tool. The result might say, for example, “spend 60% on Instagram, 30% on Google Search ads, 10% on niche blog ads” because that yields the best reach.
If multiple ad creatives (A/B variants) are to be tested, the strategy might allocate, say, 70% of budget to the top variant and 30% to a second variant to gather data (this mimics A/B testing allocation, which could be a simple rule or a multi-armed bandit algorithm for bonus complexity).
This step adds a data-driven rigor to the campaign. It’s not just creating ads, but also answering “what’s the plan to use these ads effectively?” The output could be a mini media plan: listing each ad copy with a recommended channel, target audience details (from knowledge graph, e.g. age 20-30 fitness enthusiasts), and budget or schedule. It demonstrates use of optimization techniques beyond generative AI.
Multimodal Output (Voice & More): Now for the grand finale of the demo, the system takes one of the flagship ad copies (perhaps the one intended for a video or radio spot) and runs it through ElevenLabs to generate a voiceover. For example, if a 15-second radio ad script was generated, you can click “Play” and let the audience hear “You push your limits – your gear should too. Try EcoRun Shoes... [spoken in a clear, energetic voice]”. This instant text-to-speech conversion will likely impress the hackathon judges, showing the campaign is “ready to go” across formats. Additionally, if image generation was included, show the AI-created banner or social post image alongside the text and voice – a complete creative package.
Optionally, if time permits, wrap these outputs in a simple UI: e.g. a demo web app that, after input, displays the generated ads, maybe with an interface to preview the voice or download assets. The UI could simulate a mini dashboard where the user can toggle through the suggested ad copies, listen to audio, and see recommended budget distribution (perhaps a small chart showing spend per channel). This isn’t strictly required, but a clean presentation helps convey the “instant campaign wizard” feel.
Iteration (Interactive Refinement): Though the hackathon demo might end with one pass, you can mention that the user could refine the campaign in subsequent rounds. For instance, the user could say “Tone it more playful” or “Add a discount offer” and the system (thanks to the agent design) can loop back: the BusinessAnalysisAgent updates the context (e.g. note a 10% discount) and the ContentGenerationAgent regenerates variants with that change. This shows the flexibility of the approach – because the agents are modular, the system can easily be adjusted to new goals or feedback, rather than one big black-box prompt. It highlights the advantage of your agentic, structured approach over naive prompting
medium.com
.
Best Tech Setup to Leverage These Components
To implement the above, here’s a proposed tech stack and approach leveraging your available resources:
Google ADK with Gemini: Use Google’s Agent Development Kit (ADK) to define the multi-agent workflow. ADK natively supports connecting to the Gemini LLM and managing agent dialogues. The MCP (Machine Conversation Protocol) server can be deployed (e.g. on a Vultr instance or Cloud Run) to facilitate communication between the agents and tools. This gives you a robust framework to build the orchestrator and specialized agents without starting from scratch. The Medium example project shows that ADK + Gemini handled multi-turn chat and content generation effectively

Snowflake for Data + RelationalAI (if available): Load any structured data you have into Snowflake. For example, if you can get a dataset of advertising benchmarks or social media audience stats, put it in Snowflake tables. Use Snowflake Snowpark (Python) to run queries from your app. If you want to incorporate a knowledge graph, you could use an external graph DB (Neo4j) or try Snowflake’s integration with RelationalAI to create a graph model on top of your data

Instant Magic Demo Scenario
In the demo, you’ll want to emphasize how quickly and intelligently the system produces a full campaign. Here’s how you might present it:
Setup: “Imagine I’m a startup founder with a cool new product but no marketing team. I enter a one-liner about my business into our app… hit ‘Generate Campaign’ … and watch this.”
Live Generation: Show on the screen (or explain verbally) how the AI agents are working: “First, the AI analyzes my business and audience – it’s pulling in facts like ‘our shoes are made from recycled bottles’ and learning that our target audience cares about sustainability and running performance. Next, it’s generating tailored ads for multiple platforms… and now it’s figuring out the best way to spend my $10k budget across those platforms.” You don’t want the audience to wait too long, so if the generation takes e.g. ~30 seconds, you can fill the time by describing these steps or have a progress bar with labels (“Analyzing… Generating copy… Optimizing plan…”).
Results Display: Then, show the outputs:
A selection of ad copies: e.g. “Ad Headline: Run Green, Run Strong. – Text: You push your limits and so do we… [plus maybe hashtags]”. Maybe show 2-3 variants with notes like “(Recommended)” on the best one.
The strategy recommendation: e.g. a small table or chart: “Instagram – $6,000 – 50k reach; Google Ads – $3,000 – 30k reach; Niche Running Blogs – $1,000 – 5k reach”, along with the rationale that younger audiences are on Instagram (the AI figured this out from the knowledge graph data). This highlights that the tool doesn’t just write ads, it also gives you a media plan.
A button or audio player for the ElevenLabs voice ad. Play it for the audience – that will likely get smiles and really drive home the “we even made the ad for you!” point. (Be sure to test the audio output beforehand and have speakers set up.)
Explanation of Tech: After the “wow” moment, briefly mention how it worked under the hood, to appeal to tech-savvy judges. For example: “Underneath, we have a Gemini LLM handling the language generation, but it’s not just one prompt – we use a multi-agent system where one agent gathered all the important facts from our Snowflake knowledge base (sort of a mini knowledge graph of the product) and from similar successful ads (using vector embeddings to find related content). Then another agent crafted the copy, and yet another solved an optimization model to decide the budget split. We even integrated ElevenLabs for voice and tapped Solana blockchain to log the campaign data for transparency. All these pieces come together seamlessly to deliver a campaign in one go.” This description shows you used each resource thoughtfully (Gemini for text, Snowflake for data/graph, embeddings for context, optimization for strategy, ElevenLabs for voice, Solana for an extra innovation).
Instant Adjustments (if interactive): You could further impress by adjusting the input and regenerating to show versatility. For instance: “Let’s say now I target an older demographic on a larger budget.” Change a parameter and run again to show the outputs adapt (ads might become more formal, channel mix might shift to Facebook or email). This reinforces the power of the system’s design – because it’s grounded in data and knowledge, it can pivot to new scenarios with minimal tweaks.
By combining knowledge graphs for brand context, embedding-driven retrieval of examples, deterministic optimization for planning, and multi-agent LLM orchestration, this project demonstrates a cutting-edge approach to automating ad campaigns.
